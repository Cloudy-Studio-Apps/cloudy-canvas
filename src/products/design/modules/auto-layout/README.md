# Auto layout

Figma's auto layout: frames that lay out their children in a row, column or grid, kept laid out after every edit, with Shift+A to add it and the Auto layout and Resizing sections to tune it.

- **Tier:** core
- **Requires:** core, frame; after: variables
- **Contributes:** designCanvas: `afterEdit`, `hostCommands`, `onMoved`; designEditor: `contextMenu`, `mainMenu`, `propertySections`, `sessionHooks`; `designMcpTools`
- **Points it owns:** —
- **MCP tools:** `add_auto_layout`, `remove_auto_layout`
- **Files:** `relayout.ts` (the `afterEdit` pass), `layout-frame.ts`/`layout-items.ts`/`axis-items.ts` (the layout), `layout-drop.ts` (dropping into a frame), `add-auto-layout.ts`, `guess-layout.ts`, `auto-layout.mcp.ts`, `ui/` (sections).

## How it works

- **Auto layout** (`auto-layout/`, Figma's): `FrameElement.layout` is an `AutoLayout` (`core/node/node-layout.types.ts`: `direction`, `wrap`, `gap`, `counterGap`, `padding` (`BoxSides`), `align` (`start`/`center`/`end`/`space-between`, Figma's Auto gap), `counterAlign` (with `baseline`: texts' first lines, `kind.baseline`), `strokesIncluded` (the frame's own strokes' inside part adds to its padding, CSS's `border-box`, as Figma's `bordersTakeSpace`), `reverseStacking` (first layer drawn on top: `treeOrderKeys` gives those siblings reversed ranks)) or `null`; every node has `sizing` (`NodeSizing { h, v }`: `fixed`, `hug`, `fill`), `layoutAbsolute` and `sizeLimits`. Kinds say `hugs(el, axis)` (a frame with a layout and hug sizing; a text by its `autoResize`, which is its hugging, so texts store only fixed/fill), `canHug`, `sizingOf` and `sizingPatch` (the text's maps hugging to `autoResize`, `autoResizeFor`). `layoutFrame(input, items)` is the pure layout (`axis-items.ts`: equal fill shares within limits; wrapped lines; hug sizes, clamped), fed by `frameLayoutInput` (visible, non-absolute children in `index` order with their boxes in the frame's own space, `toFrameSpace`: unturned and un-mirrored, so a mirrored frame's layout runs right to left on screen; groups by their resolved bounds and turned layers are only moved). `relayoutAfterEdit` is the design's `afterEdit`: `dirtyFrames` (layout frames edited, or above an edit of a layout field, climbing through layout frames and child hosts; a moved-out child's old parent from `before`) laid out deepest first through a `DocumentTree` (fresh reads, a parent index from `doc.field`), resizing with `kind.resize` (keeping `sizing` and the node's origin: a mirrored one grows from its right edge) and moving with descendants; imports keep Figma's positions until something is edited. `DesignNodeKind.resize` (by hand or an agent's `w`/`h`) makes the changed sides fixed (kinds resize their shape in `resizeShape`). `reorderDropped` (an `onMoved`) gives a layer dropped in a layout frame the index of the place under its center. `addAutoLayout` (Shift+A: frames without one get `guessLayout`: direction, median gap, padding, alignment from their layers; anything else is wrapped in a new hugging frame, ordered by position) and `removeAutoLayout` (Alt+Shift+A: hug and fill become fixed) are host commands (`useAutoLayoutCommands`) in the main menu and context menu. UI: the Auto layout section (`AutoLayoutFields`: direction/wrap, `AlignmentGrid`, gap with Auto, line gap, padding pairs, strokes included, first on top) and the Resizing section (W/H Fixed/Hug/Fill, Ignore auto layout). Agents: `layout` (`readAutoLayout`, merged over the current one; `null` removes it), `sizing` (`readSizing`; hugging only where `kind.hugs` allows), `layoutAbsolute` and `sizeLimits` in `create_nodes`/`update_nodes` (`readNodeLayoutFields`), `add_auto_layout`/`remove_auto_layout`; summaries carry `layout`, `sizing` and `layoutAbsolute`. The Figma import maps `stack*` fields (`fig-auto-layout.ts`: a missing primary sizing is hug, a missing counter sizing fixed; `stackChildPrimaryGrow`/`stackChildAlignSelf: STRETCH` fill, `stackPositioning: ABSOLUTE`); the layout matches Figma's stored positions in 626 of 648 frames of the demo files within 0.5 px (the rest are instances whose stored sizes disagree with each other).
